# Vendor, Supply Chain, and Product Quality Policy

Source ID: VENDOR-QUALITY-04  
Applies to: Purchase receipts, short shipments, vendor fill rate, damaged goods, sports equipment quality, safety complaints, and recall handling

## Short Shipments

A short shipment exists when received quantity is less than ordered quantity.

If received quantity is below 90 percent of ordered quantity for a high-demand sports item, classify as P2 and route to Supply Chain for vendor follow-up.

If the same vendor and SKU have short shipments on two consecutive receipts, recommend vendor claim review and replenishment risk summary.

The assistant should cite receipt data and vendor fill rate when explaining likely root cause.

## Vendor Performance

Vendor fill rate below 80 percent or on-time rate below 85 percent is a warning signal. It is not proof of fault by itself.

Use vendor performance data to prioritize investigation, not to accuse the vendor.

## Product Quality Complaints

Repeated complaints about cracked bats, split football seams, warped hockey sticks, torn gloves, missing protective padding, unstable treadmill parts, or unsafe sports goods within 30 days should be escalated.

If customer injury, recall notice, suspected unsafe equipment, or demo fitness equipment malfunction is reported, classify as P1. Quarantine affected stock and notify Store Manager plus Product Quality.

If quality complaints are repeated but no immediate safety risk is reported, classify as P2 and recommend vendor/product review.

## Damaged Goods and Claims

For damaged goods on receipt, record store ID, SKU, vendor ID, PO quantity, received quantity, damage notes, and photos if available. Manager or Supply Chain approval is required before claim submission.

The assistant may prepare a claim summary but must not submit a real claim unless explicitly integrated and human-approved.

## Recall and Safety

Any recall or suspected unsafe product requires immediate human escalation. The assistant should advise removing product from sale and escalating to Product Quality. It must not decide independently that a product is safe.
